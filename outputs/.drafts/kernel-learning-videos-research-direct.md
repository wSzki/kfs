## Bounded assembly bridge: nanobyte (not the kernel recipe)

- Discovery: the independently curated https://github.com/leap0x7b/awesome-osdev links nanobyte's series as a from-scratch OS and bootloader route. The creator's https://nanobyte.dev/building-an-os/ explicitly labels its new articles an improved text version and points to original video transcripts.
- Primary video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9t-SPC7Tczc — “Building an OS - 1 - Hello world,” channel nanobyte; 2019-01-26; 23:18. Description says it writes an assembly Hello World booted from floppy, with C planned for later.
- Creator's almost-verbatim transcript, with acknowledged small clarity changes: https://nanobyte.dev/transcripts/building-an-os-1-hello-world/ . Original auto captions also fetched.
- Evidence of explicit beginner vocabulary: defines assembly as human-readable machine code; mnemonic and operand; instruction versus assembler directive; `org` versus where firmware actually loads code; `bits 16` versus changing CPU execution mode; registers; label/address versus memory contents (`mov ax,var` compared with `mov ax,[var]`); and stack/call/return before the printing function. It narrates building and testing in QEMU, then debugging an omitted loop jump.
- The original description credits the x86-register diagram; the spoken transcript says a register diagram is shown. The creator transcript includes that diagram and memory-segment diagrams. This supports use of diagrams, NOT an independent judgment of video readability or animation quality.
- Important mismatch: a custom 16-bit BIOS bootsector with BIOS text interrupts, not a GRUB-loaded 32-bit C kernel. Do not recommend it as the learner's kernel build recipe or transplant `org 0x7C00`, real-mode segment arithmetic, BIOS interrupts, or 16-bit register choices into their existing lab.
- Teaching-fit inference: this has stronger evidence of introducing unfamiliar vocabulary before use than the exploratory Bare Bones stream. It can be an optional short assembly-vocabulary bridge ONLY if the final answer plainly says no strong zero-assembly exact-match course was verified.
- Technical caveat: auto captions invert `mov` direction and render stack ordering as “first in first out,” while the creator's edited transcript and examples correctly say source on right/destination on left and first-in-last-out. Do not silently treat edited transcript as exact original speech; technical authority and caption correctness remain caveated.
- No verified chapter timestamps, so do not invent a short cut point. The 23:18 runtime is verified, but a 5-minute segment boundary is not.
